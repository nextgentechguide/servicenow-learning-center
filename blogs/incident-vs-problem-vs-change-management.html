Incident, Problem, and Change Management: Understanding the Differences

Learn how incidents, problems, and changes serve different purposes and how to recognize which process a situation may require.

ServiceNow learning6 min read

Incident, problem, and change management are closely related in IT service operations, but they answer different questions. Understanding the distinction helps teams respond to disruptions, investigate recurring issues, and make improvements without introducing unnecessary risk.

Incident management: restore the service

An incident is an unplanned interruption to a service or a reduction in its quality. Incident management focuses on restoring normal service as quickly as practical and keeping affected users informed. For example, if employees cannot access an internal application, the support team records the impact, investigates the immediate symptoms, and works toward restoring access.

Problem management: understand the cause

A problem is the underlying cause, or potential cause, of one or more incidents. Problem management looks beyond the immediate interruption. If the same application outage happens repeatedly, a problem investigation may look for a common technical or process cause and identify a longer-term response.

Change management: control modifications

In modern IT service management, the practice is often described as change enablement. It helps organizations assess, authorize, schedule, and review changes to services and infrastructure. A change might be needed to apply a permanent fix, upgrade a component, or reduce a known risk. The process considers potential impact and appropriate safeguards.

How the three practices work together

  • An incident is logged when a service is disrupted.
  • The support team works to restore service and records relevant findings.
  • If similar incidents recur, a problem record may be used to investigate the underlying cause.
  • If a modification is needed, a change is assessed and handled through the organization's change process.
  • After implementation, teams monitor the outcome and update relevant records or knowledge.

This is a simplified example. Organizations may connect these records and processes differently depending on their operating model and policies.

Questions that help distinguish them

  • Is the immediate goal to restore a disrupted service? Think incident.
  • Is the goal to identify or manage the underlying cause? Think problem.
  • Does the proposed solution modify a service or environment? Consider the change process.

Why this distinction matters when learning ServiceNow

ServiceNow learners may encounter separate record types and workflows for these practices. Knowing the purpose of each process makes it easier to understand why information is captured, why tasks are assigned, and why approvals or reviews may be required.

Final thoughts

Incidents focus on service restoration, problems focus on causes, and changes focus on controlled modifications. The practices complement one another, but they are not interchangeable. Learning the difference is a useful foundation for understanding ITSM workflows.
