ServiceNow Platform Basics: How Tables, Forms, and Records Fit Together

Get familiar with three foundational platform concepts—tables, records, and forms—and see how they relate in everyday ServiceNow work.

ServiceNow learning5 min read

When starting with ServiceNow, the platform interface can feel unfamiliar because many tasks involve structured information. Tables, records, and forms are three foundational concepts that help explain how that information is organized and used.

What is a table?

A table stores a particular type of information in a structured format. You can think of it as a collection of related records, with fields defining the kinds of information each record can hold. For example, a service process may use a table to organize records about requests or incidents.

What is a record?

A record is one individual entry in a table. In a familiar spreadsheet analogy, a record is similar to a row, while its fields are similar to columns. Each record represents one item being tracked, such as a particular request or incident.

What is a form?

A form is a way to view or work with an individual record. It presents fields so a user can read information or, when permitted, create or update it. The fields shown and the actions available can depend on configuration, user roles, and the context of the task.

How they work together

Imagine a support team tracking reports about an application. The relevant table holds the collection of records. Each report is stored as an individual record, and a form gives a support agent a convenient way to review details such as the short description, status, priority, and assigned group.

Why lists are useful too

A list displays multiple records from a table so users can review, filter, sort, and open the items they need. Lists are useful when a team needs to scan a queue of work, while forms are useful for examining or updating one record at a time.

What beginners should practise

  • Navigate to a permitted list and identify the records shown.
  • Open a record and recognize its fields.
  • Notice which fields are required and which are read-only.
  • Use available filters to narrow a list.
  • Understand how permissions affect what you can view or change.

Practise in an authorized learning instance and follow the guidance for that environment. Avoid changing records in a live business system unless you have permission and understand the impact.

Final thoughts

Tables organize information, records represent individual entries, and forms provide a way to work with those entries. Once these ideas are clear, many other platform concepts become easier to understand.
